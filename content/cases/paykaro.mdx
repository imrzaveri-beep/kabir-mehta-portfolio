## Problem

A first payment should feel like a small win. In this fictional case, UPI activation was stuck at 41%. People reached the bank-linking step and left, often without knowing whether their money or account was safe. Seven separate screens asked them to make decisions before they understood the payoff.

The brief was not to make onboarding look shorter. It was to help someone complete a first transaction with confidence.

## Research

The supplied demo scenario includes 18 interviews with new and recently inactive users. The recurring questions were practical: “Which bank should I choose?”, “Why does this need my phone number?” and “Did that actually work?”

I grouped the findings into three needs: less repetition, a clear reason for each permission, and visible confirmation after every important step. A journey map made the gap between technical success and perceived success explicit.

## Ideation

I explored a single long form, a guided conversation and a three-stage flow. The long form reduced taps but increased uncertainty; the guided flow felt reassuring but slow. The final direction grouped tasks around outcomes: identify the account, connect the bank, make the first payment.

Smart bank-detection became a suggestion with an easy override, never an unexplained automatic choice. Error states were sketched alongside the happy path.

## Design

The concept uses progressive disclosure and short, specific explanations. Users see their chosen account before committing, can change the bank without restarting, and receive a clear success state after linking.

The original mockups below illustrate hierarchy and flow rather than a functioning payment app. Components include accessible input states, connection feedback and a compact receipt. No payments or financial data are collected by this portfolio.

## Results

In the fictional brief, activation rises from 41% to 79% after six weeks: **38 percentage points**, not a 38% relative increase. The distinction matters when discussing an outcome. Onboarding falls from seven steps to three.

These numbers are supplied demo metrics, not independently measured results. A real rollout would compare cohorts and watch unsuccessful links, support contacts and first-payment completion alongside activation.

## Key learnings

- Removing a screen does not automatically remove doubt.
- Suggestions work best when people can correct them.
- Measure confidence as well as completion.
- Define the metric before celebrating the change.
