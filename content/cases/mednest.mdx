## Problem

Clinic staff should spend their attention on patients, not on navigating a busy dashboard. In this demo scenario, booking an appointment required moving between disconnected views while the queue continued to grow.

The challenge was to make scheduling faster without hiding the context needed to avoid mistakes.

## Research

The illustrative research plan follows front-desk tasks: identify the patient, find an appropriate slot, confirm the visit and handle changes. A task map highlights repeated data entry and points where staff must remember information between screens.

The assumptions in this fictional case need validation with real clinic teams. No actual patient data appears in the mockups.

## Ideation

I explored a calendar-first workspace, a patient-first search and a unified daily queue. The daily view provided the best starting point for common tasks, while search stayed available for returning patients.

Early sketches separated urgent alerts from routine status. Colour was paired with text so that state would not depend on colour recognition.

## Design

A consistent appointment panel keeps patient context, time and visit details together. The daily dashboard offers a clear queue, visible status and direct paths to the next task. Confirmation screens repeat the critical details before saving.

The mockups use invented names and appointment entries. This portfolio includes no clinic backend, medical records or real scheduling functionality.

## Results

The client-supplied fictional outcome is **3× faster appointment booking**. A real benchmark should time the same task, separate new and returning patients, and measure booking errors alongside speed.

The design deliberately leaves an explicit confirmation step. Saving a few seconds is not worth creating an incorrect appointment.

## Key learnings

- Put high-frequency tasks where the work already happens.
- Faster is useful only when it is also correct.
- State needs words as well as colour.
- Design the interrupted workflow, not just the ideal one.
